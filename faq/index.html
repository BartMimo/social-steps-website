Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Social Steps.

How does Social Steps work?

Social Steps reads your daily steps from Apple Health and converts them into screen-time minutes. You choose which apps to limit; those apps stay blocked until you've earned enough time by walking.

How many steps do I need for one minute?

By default, 200 steps equal 1 minute of screen time. With Premium you can set your own conversion rate to make it easier or more challenging.

Which apps can I block?

You can select any apps or categories on your device using Apple's Screen Time picker. Social Steps never sees the names of the apps you choose — that selection stays private to your device.

Do I need to keep the app open?

No. The blocking runs in the background through Apple's Screen Time system. Open Social Steps now and then so it can update your latest step count.

Why does my step count show 0?

Social Steps needs permission to read your steps. Go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Social Steps and turn on Steps. You can also tap “Open Settings” on the app's home screen.

Why is the counter only updated when I reopen the app?

Apple limits how often apps can read step data in the background. When you return to Social Steps it refreshes immediately, and it also corrects past days using your real total from Apple Health.

What does Premium include?

Premium lets you block whole categories, like all social media at once, and as many apps as you want (the free plan blocks up to 3 single apps). You can block only during the hours you choose, turn on strict mode (no bonus minutes or removing apps, and switching it off only takes effect at midnight) and see a weekly overview compared with last week. You can also remove a blocked app daily instead of once a week, set your own conversion rate and add bonus minutes manually. It's a monthly or yearly subscription, billed through your App Store account in your local currency. If you're eligible for a free trial, the App Store shows it before you confirm.

What do I get with a friend's invite code?

Enter a valid code from a friend in the app (during setup or in Settings) and you get one month of Premium for free, once per iCloud account. The friend who invited you gets a free month too (up to 12 months in total), the next time they open the app. It ends automatically and nothing is charged.

How do I cancel my subscription?

Open the Settings app on your iPhone, tap your name › Subscriptions › Social Steps, and choose Cancel. Your Premium features remain active until the end of the current billing period.

Is my health data safe?

Yes. Your steps are read on your device and are never sold or used for ads. Only new step counts are added, without your name, to the community total in iCloud; your history stays on your device. Read more in our Privacy Policy.

Does Social Steps work on Android?

Yes. The Android app is coming to Google Play and works on Android 9 or later. It works the same as on iPhone: you walk, earn minutes and choose which apps stay locked. Your steps come from Health Connect instead of Apple Health.

How does the Android app read my steps?

Through Health Connect, with your permission. Social Steps only reads your step count, also in the background so a blocked app unlocks as soon as you've walked enough. Your history stays on your phone. You can turn access off in Health Connect › App permissions › Social Steps. More in our Privacy Policy.

Why does the Android app need the accessibility service?

Android doesn't let apps block other apps the way Apple's Screen Time does, so Social Steps uses the accessibility service to notice when you open an app on your block list. It only sees which app is opened. It does not read your screen, what you type or your messages, and it collects nothing. If you turn it off, blocking stops.

How do I cancel on Android?

Open the Google Play Store app, tap your profile › Payments & subscriptions › Subscriptions › Social Steps, and choose Cancel. Premium stays active until the end of the current period.

It doesn't work on my work/managed iPhone.

Some devices managed by an employer or organization (MDM) restrict Screen Time permissions, which Social Steps needs to block apps. On a personal iPhone this works normally.
